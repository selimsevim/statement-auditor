Statement Auditor

Scores public corporate modern-slavery statements on disclosure quality — how specific and substantiated they are — and catches when a company just re-files last year's.

Automated analysis of public statements — not a legal assessment. It measures statement quality and disclosure specificity. It never asserts that any company uses forced labour.

The problem

The law requires a statement.
It doesn't grade one.

  • Every large UK company must publish a modern-slavery statement. Thousands do — the registry holds 100,000+.
  • Nobody reads them at scale, and the Act doesn't measure quality.
  • So a company can file four pages of boilerplate, or re-file last year's, and tick the box.
UK Modern Slavery Act 2015 — s.54(5)

Six recommended content areas

Structure & supply chains · Policies · Due diligence · Risk assessment · Effectiveness & KPIs · Training.

The Act recommends what to disclose but sets no bar for how well. That gap is what we score.

What it scores

Seven dimensions, anchored to the Act

  • 1–6 map directly to s.54(5)'s six content areas, each scored 0–4 against our rubric.
  • 7 · Boilerplate is computed, not LLM-scored — year-over-year paragraph reuse.
  • Overall = mean of dimensions 1–6, penalised by boilerplate.
  • If >60% of paragraphs are carried over, the overall is capped at 2.0 and flagged.

How it works

Two LLM passes, never one giant prompt

PASS 1 · EXTRACT

Claims

Claude Haiku 4.5 pulls verbatim claims per dimension into strict JSON. PDFs read column-aware with PyMuPDF so quotes stay intact.

PASS 2 · SCORE

Rubric 0–4

Claude Sonnet 5 scores each dimension, sampled 3× with the median taken. Every cited quote is verified verbatim in the source — or dropped.

COMPUTED

Boilerplate diff

Paragraph embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2), year-over-year cosine similarity at a 0.95 calibrated threshold, plus hedge-word density.

All LLM responses are cached on (model, prompt hash) — re-runs are free and the committed snapshot is deterministic.

Evidence you can trust

Every score cites the source's own words

  • Each dimension expands to its justification and verbatim evidence — the exact span sliced from the PDF text.
  • The scoring model can't invent a quote: if a cited quote doesn't string-match the source, we drop it.
  • If a dimension loses all its evidence, it's scored 0. No hallucinated praise.
  • The dashboard highlights by stored character offset — never re-matched in the browser.
"…verified to appear verbatim in the extracted source text, highlighted by the character offset stored at scoring time."
✓ source-verified quote dropped if unmatched no evidence → 0

A reviewer opening the original PDF finds the exact sentence — cosmetic differences (case, bullets, hyphenation) tolerated, wording never paraphrased.
